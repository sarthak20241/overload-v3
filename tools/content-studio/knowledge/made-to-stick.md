# Sticky ideas: the working rules

A summary in our own words of the framework in *Made to Stick* (Chip Heath and
Dan Heath). Every topic and every post is checked against it.

## The enemy: the curse of knowledge

Once you know something, you cannot remember what it felt like not to know it.
A founder who has lived inside the app for a year writes in feature names,
internal words and abstractions ("adaptive progression engine"). The reader
has none of that context. Every rule below is a way to undo this curse: write
for the person who has never heard of Overload.

## Commander's intent

Before writing, finish this sentence: "If the reader remembers only one thing,
it is ___." One post carries one idea. Everything that does not serve that one
idea gets cut, even if it is true and interesting.

## S: Simple

- Find the core. Strip the idea down until it is short AND still deep.
- Prefer one sharp point over three good ones.
- Use what the reader already knows: an analogy or a comparison ("a trainer
  sees you twice a week, this one sees every set") explains faster than a
  definition.

## U: Unexpected

- Get attention by breaking a pattern the reader expects. Say the thing that
  sounds wrong at first and is right on reflection.
- Keep attention with a curiosity gap: show the reader there is something they
  do not know yet, then close the gap later in the post.
- Surprise must serve the core message. A gimmick that is not tied to the
  point is noise.

## C: Concrete

- Use things a reader can see, hear or picture: a specific set, a weight, a
  screen, a day, a bug.
- Numbers beat adjectives. "Plan generation went from 30s to 13s" beats
  "much faster".
- Show the real thing: a screenshot, a before and after, the actual message
  the coach sent.

## C: Credible

- Borrow credibility from detail, not from claims. Vivid, specific detail
  makes a story believable.
- Make statistics human-sized ("that is one extra rep every week for a year").
- Let the reader test it: "open your last 4 weeks of logs and check your top
  set" is stronger than "trust me".
- Pass the hard case: if it works in the toughest situation, it works
  everywhere.

## E: Emotional

- People care about one person, not about an average. Write about one lifter,
  one moment, one feeling.
- Tie the idea to what the reader wants for themselves and who they want to
  be (the lifter who keeps getting stronger, the founder who ships).
- Benefits over features. Feelings over specs.

## S: Stories

Stories are a flight simulator: they show the reader what to do and make them
want to do it. Three plots work again and again:

- **Challenge**: an underdog beats an obstacle (a solo founder, a stalled
  lifter who breaks a plateau).
- **Connection**: people bridge a gap (a user message that changed the
  roadmap).
- **Creativity**: someone solves a problem in a new way (a bug fixed by
  thinking about it from the lifter's side).

## Quick check before posting

1. Can I say the one idea in one sentence?
2. What in the first line would make a stranger stop?
3. What can the reader picture?
4. Why should they believe it?
5. Why should they care, personally?
6. Is there a person or a moment in it?

A post does not need all six. It needs a clear core plus at least three of the
others done well.
